The data: raster layers and one polygon per plot.

Plotline reads three kinds of input: the 17 supplier plots in the SystemEarth sample, a tree-cover loss map from the University of Maryland (Hansen) and a 2020 forest map from the EU's Joint Research Centre (JRC).

25 min readLevel: deep diveSources: docs/research/company-sample-audit.md, docs/research/layer-acquisition.md, docs/lab/engine-data-W4.md, docs/lab/decision-scale-notes.md
In brief
  • A satellite map here is a grid of whole numbers. The Hansen chip is 1,233 × 604 = 744,732 cells, three numbers per cell, about 28 m wide.
  • Of the Hansen chip's 576,999 bytes, the canopy percentage band is 72%, and no EUDR question reads it.
  • SystemEarth's sample is 17 spreadsheet rows and 17 outlines that join one-to-one. A plot owns a map cell when the cell's centre is inside its outline.
1 · Two ways to draw a map

A raster is a grid with a number in every cell

A raster is a rectangular grid of cells, each holding a number. A satellite "image" used for science is usually not a photo: each cell stores a measurement, and a colour is chosen later to display it.

A vector describes shapes by their corner points. A farm boundary is a vector polygon, a closed ring of points. The raster says what each patch of ground looks like; the polygon says which patches to read.

A map can hold several aligned measurements per cell, each a band. The panel shows three bands on a synthetic landscape shaped like the Hansen bands.

Whole numbers in one byte: uint8

Each Hansen cell is a uint8, an unsigned 8-bit integer: one byte, a whole number from 0 to 255. Hansen uses only part of that range.

Each bit adds its weight.0

Source: docs/explainer/01-maps-as-numbers.html and the Hansen band codes in docs/research/layer-acquisition.md. One uncompressed band of the chip would be 744,732 bytes (one per cell); the real files are smaller because they are compressed.

Nodata

Nodata means "no usable observation". It is not zero. Hansen's lossyear is 0 for "no mapped loss", so a second band, datamask, is needed to know whether that zero was observed. In the chip, datamask 0 would mean no data, 1 mapped land, 2 permanent water. No cell in the chip has mask 0, but 56 cells on permanent water carry a non-zero loss year, which is why Plotline reads the mask first. Unknown never becomes "clear".

2 · Georeferencing

A GeoTIFF is a raster with location metadata

A GeoTIFF is an image file with extra metadata. The numbers are the bands. The metadata gives the coordinate system and how a row and column become a place on Earth. These maps use WGS 84 (EPSG:4326): longitude and latitude in degrees.

The link between "row 3, column 5" and a longitude and latitude is an affine transform: start at a known corner, then take one fixed step per column and one per row. The origin is invented for illustration; the step matches Hansen's real 0.00025° (0.9 arc-seconds). Click a cell, or focus the grid and use the arrow keys.

Tile and chip

A tile is one section of the world map: Hansen publishes the world in 10° × 10° tiles. At 0.00025° per cell, one tile is 40,000 × 40,000 = 1.6 billion cells. A chip is a small cut-out of a tile; the one used here covers the bounding box of the 17 plots and keeps the original cells exactly, with no resampling. Tile 10N_090E spans 90–100°E and 0–10°N and contains the sample (near 99°E).

Inside a TIFF file, "tile" also means a block of bytes: a storage tile is a unit of packing, unrelated to a geographic tile.

3 · The company sample

17 spreadsheet rows, 17 outlines, joined by plot ID.

SystemEarth supplied two small files for one shipment lot from one supplier.

  • A CSV with 17 data rows and 35 columns: one supplier due-diligence declaration per plot.
  • A GeoJSON file with 17 matching plot polygons.

The spreadsheet column a3_plot_id matches the GeoJSON property ProductionPlace one-to-one for all 17 plots, with no repeated, missing or unmatched IDs. The plot IDs are P-0001, P-0003 to P-0006, P-0008 to P-0011 and P-0013 to P-0020. The gaps (P-0002, P-0007, P-0012) are not treated as missing records unless a manifest says they were expected.

The CSV labels every row Chai Buri, Surat Thani, Thailand. The plots sit within a box of roughly 34 × 17 km around 99° E, 8.5° N, consistent with that label, which was not checked against administrative boundaries. The plots are smallholdings of 0.53 to 20.07 ha.

Two cautions. Hansen loss is not legal deforestation: it records tree-cover loss from any cause, including harvest, fire or storms, and does not say what the land became. Also, treecover2000 is the canopy in the year 2000; subtracting the loss from it does not give "forest in 2020".

Go deeper: 72% and 75% technical

72% is treecover2000's share of the three GeoTIFF files (414,142 of 576,999 bytes). 75% is its share when each band is compressed with the same yardstick (zstd level 19 of the raw plane: 397,973 B). The yardstick compares attributes fairly; file sizes are what is on disk. This page uses file sizes, and 75% appears elsewhere in the pack.

5 · JRC Global Forest Cover 2020

The JRC 2020 forest map: forest or not, on a finer grid

The EU Joint Research Centre publishes a global forest cover map for 2020. Each cell is forest or not. It supplies the baseline the EUDR question needs: what was forest at the end of 2020. Plotline uses version 4, one band of uint8, WGS 84, free to use with acknowledgement.

  • Different grid. JRC cells are 1/12,000° wide, about 9 m, against Hansen's 28 m. The JRC chip (3,695 × 1,809 cells) is kept as a separate layer on its own grid.
  • One year only. It describes 2020: no time series, nothing about later years.
  • Codes. 1 is forest. 0 appears in the data but the catalogue lists only code 1, so 0 is treated as "not mapped as forest" and the raw value is kept. In the chip, 1,066,195 of 6,684,255 cells are code 1: about 16%.

The JRC step is a third of the Hansen step (0.00025°), so a 3 × 3 block of JRC cells sits exactly inside one Hansen cell. Each of the nine cells in the panel can be clicked to flip it.

Limited testNo plot decision changes under either alternative, but only one plot (P-0001) has any mapped forest, so the test is weak. The other 16 have no forest cell under any rule.

Why both maps are needed

Hansen alone would count a plantation cut and replanted since 2020 as "loss", but the EUDR forest definition excludes plantations. Across the whole chip, Hansen shows 80,429 land cells with loss after 2020; only 341 (0.42%) sit on JRC-mapped 2020 forest. The gap shows why a second layer matters; it does not establish the cause of any loss.

How the JRC layer was acquired

The official v3 tile request returned HTTP 500 on retry, so v3 was never acquired and nothing is claimed from it. The official per-tile file of v4 worked: a 197,739,699-byte tile (120,000 × 120,000 cells), from which a 46,238-byte chip was cut that matches the source window exactly. v3 and v4 are not interchangeable, so the data is labelled v4. The JRC v4 technical report is not yet published (expected by December 2026).

Go deeper: the fringe and the 3 × 3 arithmetic technical

The JRC chip stops a little short of the Hansen chip: 3 × 1,233 is 3,699 columns but the chip is 3,695 wide. A one-row, two-column fringe (2,439 cells) has no JRC value. There the forest state is marked unknown rather than guessed, and every plot in the sample lies inside the covered area.

6 · Which cells belong to a plot?

A cell belongs to a plot if its centre is inside the outline.

The centre-inclusion rule: a whole cell is kept when its centre is inside the polygon or exactly on its edge. Touching a cell's corner is not enough.

The outline in the panel can be dragged, or focused and moved with the arrow keys. It snaps to quarter-cells, so an edge can sit exactly on a row of centres. The shapes are synthetic.

Source: rule hansen-centers.v1 (docs/HANDOVER.md) and docs/explainer/02-answering-a-plot.html. This widget uses browser JavaScript to illustrate it; the engine uses Shapely's covers, which is boundary-inclusive. Cell counts are cells, not hectares.

  • A hole removes its inside, but its edge still counts as part of the polygon. Two parts (a MultiPolygon) select a centre once if it is in either.
  • A tiny plot can select no centres at all. Zero selected cells means insufficient spatial support. It is never reported as "clear".
  • The rule is a documented choice. Counting any cell the outline overlaps adds 337 plot-and-cell memberships across the 17 plots and changes five period flags. Under both rules the original and compact answers are identical.
7 · Layers not used

Protected areas and other unused sources

  • WDPA (Protected Planet). The protected-areas layer would be a natural third input for legality, but its terms prohibit commercial use without permission and restrict redistribution, including in derivative works. It was not downloaded or embedded. A national protected-area source could be used once the jurisdiction and licence are confirmed.
  • JRC v3. The tile request failed; v4 replaced it.
  • Alerts. Near-real-time deforestation alerts are named in the challenge brief. None was acquired; they are review signals with false positives, and a different problem.
  • A derived 2020 forest map. Forest in 2020 was not derived by subtracting Hansen loss from 2000 canopy, because the product definition does not support that equivalence.
  • Older Hansen releases. The supplier cites "Hansen 2021-2023" in its own records. The chips are the latest release (v1.13, to 2025), so the two are not the same vintage and are never treated as one.
