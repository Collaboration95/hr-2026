Overview

Plotline overview

Plotline is a compact store for EUDR forest-loss screening. It keeps only the facts the decision reads and gives the same answers as the original map layers.

In brief
  • The problem: EUDR screening intersects farm plots with years of forest maps, and repeats the check whenever a map updates. The challenge brief asks for compact storage, fast queries and the same plot-level answer as the original layers.
  • The result: a 6.1 KB decision layer replaces 623 KB of original layers, with identical answers for all 744,732 map cells.
  • The speed: registered plots re-check 44× faster and new boundaries about 5× faster. Every answer carries a receipt.
1 · The problem

EUDR plot-level screening

Companies that sell rubber, palm oil, cocoa or coffee into the EU must show that every supplying farm plot is deforestation-free under the EU Deforestation Regulation, EUDR.

A plot's boundary is checked against satellite maps for two things: whether the land was forest on 31 December 2020, the rule's cut-off date, and whether tree cover has been lost since. The maps come from different sources at different sharpness and update every year. The check is therefore re-run for millions of plots whenever a layer updates, and the data accumulates.

The brief's slide title frames the task: "Keep the decision, not the data." The layers are to be stored compactly and queried fast while reproducing exactly the same plot-level decisions as the original layers.

2 · The data

17 supplier plots and two public maps

A raster is a map stored as a grid of squares, one number per square, like graph paper. Satellite forest maps are rasters. A farm plot is a vector: an outline traced with coordinates. The task is laying outlines over grids.

3 · Design

Plotline design: three questions, one decision layer

Plotline starts from the three questions an EUDR-style screen asks of a plot, and what each needs from the maps.

Q1

Was it forest on 31 Dec 2020?

Needs JRC's 2020 forest class, and land / water / unknown.

Q2

Has that forest been lost since?

Needs Q1 plus the Hansen loss year, 2021 to 2025.

Q3

What did the supplier's own check say?

The supplier named "Hansen Tree Cover Loss 2021-2023": any Hansen loss in 2021 to 2023, on any land.

No question reads the canopy percentages, which are 72% of Hansen's bytes, or loss years before 2021. Both are dropped. Each map cell is reduced to one of 20 codes.

The screening layer answers all three questions. The decision layer answers Q1 and Q2 only, so it also forgets the loss year on land that was not forest in 2020: loss on land the EU's 2020 forest map does not class as forest, which a Hansen-only check counts as loss. The 20 codes are below.

3 forest states × 6 loss states + water + unknown = 20 codes. The decision layer merges every N·year into plain N. Orange outline = loss after the cut-off. Unknown is its own value: a missing observation is never recorded as "no loss".

Where the last bytes come from technical

A plain GeoTIFF of the decision codes is already 8,532 B, 98.6% smaller than the originals, so most of the saving comes from deciding what to keep rather than from a codec. Plotline's encoding (separate bit-planes for land, water and forest, plus a sparse list of the few loss events) removes a further 28%, down to 6,146 B. The lossless "v1" store is retained as the audit tier in case the rule changes.

A learned codec selector lost to a fixed setting on a sealed holdout region and was rejected.

5 · The proof

Identical answers, checked for every cell

Dropping data is acceptable only if nothing the decision reads is lost. An independent path rebuilds the code for every map cell straight from the original GeoTIFFs and compares it with the decision layer. The comparison covers all 744,732 cells, not a sample.

This covers every plot, not only the 17 supplied: each answer depends only on the codes of the selected cells, so if every cell's code matches, any boundary drawn in the region gets identical outputs under the same centre rule.

Design limits

  • Edge cells are all-or-nothing. Under the centre rule a cell counts for a plot when its centre lies inside the boundary; cells are about 0.076 ha. Partial edge coverage and the 0.5 ha minimum area are not modelled.
  • JRC is sampled once per Hansen cell, at the centre of its 3 × 3 block. The alternatives (majority, any forest) give the same decision for all 17 plots, but only one plot has mapped forest.
  • The layer is rule-specific. A changed rule or an added layer means re-deriving from the lossless audit tier: seconds per region, about 7 CPU-minutes per tile (estimated).
  • The largest speed-ups need registered plots. The 44× to 410× figures apply to plots registered in advance; a new boundary gets 5.2×.
  • Storage was measured on a whole tile and query timing on one region.
Speed phases: 24× to 44× history

The decision layers were benchmarked first: registered plots 24×, new boundaries 2.2×. Four phases followed with no change to any output, store byte or file format. Phase 1 sped up decoding, histogram reduction and polygon selection (31× and 3.0×). Phase 2 added a scalar scan, a batch evaluator and the numpy-free re-check (33× and 5.0×). Phase 3 sped up the shared reducer (44×). Phase 4 trimmed it again (numpy-free 65× to 83×; new boundaries read 5.2×) and ended with a research pass that found nothing that beats this approach for small exact plots on a CPU.

8 · What it found

Findings on the 17 supplied plots

With Hansen alone, 7 of the 17 plots show tree-cover loss after 2020. Hansen records any tree-cover loss on any land, and the region is rubber and oil-palm country. With the JRC 2020 forest map added, only P-0001 has mapped loss on mapped 2020 forest: 2 cells, in 2022, out of its 38 Hansen loss cells that year.

Filter

One dot per plot; positions are decorative. The six other plots with Hansen loss after 2020 (P-0005, P-0006, P-0013, P-0014, P-0016, P-0018) had that loss only on land JRC did not map as forest in 2020. Counts are cells, not hectares.

What the supplier declared

Declared indication, no mapped loss

All 17 declare "Deforestation-free", checked against "Hansen Tree Cover Loss 2021-2023". Five (P-0003, P-0005, P-0008, P-0010, P-0013) record an "indication of deforestation", resolved by on-site verification. On that layer and the project's rule, none of those five shows loss in 2021 to 2023.

The reverse case

Declared none, map shows some

Three plots declared no indication but show 2021 to 2023 loss on the layer they cite: P-0001 (38 cells in 2022), P-0016 (1 in 2023), P-0018 (1 in 2021). Neither the older Hansen release nor a different boundary rule explains the gap.

ScopeThese are mapped observations under a written rule, not a legal compliance verdict. The wording is "mapped loss on mapped 2020 forest", not "deforestation found". The screen does not model EUDR's 0.5 ha minimum area, the "able to reach 5 m" test or protected areas.
