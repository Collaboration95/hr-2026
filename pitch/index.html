Presenting

Presentation guide for the SystemEarth finals

The finals take place on 17 October 2026. This guide covers the summary line, 30-second and 90-second versions, a timed three-minute demo, a slide outline, questions and answers, terminology and a readiness checklist.

In brief
  • Summary line: a 6 KB decision layer gives the same EUDR screening answers as 623 KB of original map layers, proven for every cell in the region.
  • Key figures: 99% smaller · 744,732 of 744,732 cells identical · 44× faster for registered plots · 5.2× for new boundaries · 98.6% (the plain-GeoTIFF control) · 2 cells in 2022 on P-0001.
  • Pitch length, format and judging weights are not confirmed. Every piece is built to be cut: 30 s, 90 s and a 3-minute demo.

Approach

Every piece can be cut

The material is a summary line, 30-second and 90-second spoken versions, a 3-minute demo and a slide outline that maps one-to-one onto the company's own evaluation measures. Whatever the slot, material is cut from the end.

Evaluation measures in the printed brief

The printed brief asks for the essential layers to be identified, for what can be simplified or dropped to be stated (grounded in the regulation), for an encoding strategy to be described, and for a sample region to be encoded and queried in a working prototype. It then lists what is measured. The proposed slide structure follows this list in order, so each measure has its own slide.

The 90-second version

The writeup's "longer version" runs about 420 words, nearer 2¾ minutes at a normal 150 words a minute than 60 to 90 seconds. The trimmed version below is about 200 words (about 80 seconds) and uses only its sentences and numbers; the longer one suits a four-minute slot.

Problem · 10 sEUDR screening is a geospatial join that gets re-run: plot boundaries, forest at the 2020 cut-off, and annual loss since, every time a layer updates.

Layer analysis · 20 sThe analysis identifies what the decision reads: is each map cell land or water, was it forest on 31 December 2020, and in what year was it lost since. Hansen's canopy percentages are 72% of its bytes, and no question reads them. All of that is dropped, and one 20-value code per cell is kept.

Proof · 20 sAn independent path rebuilds every code from the original GeoTIFFs: 744,732 of 744,732 cells identical. Every output depends only on the selected cells' codes, so every possible plot boundary gets the same answer, not just the 17 supplied plots.

Numbers · 25 s6.1 kilobytes against 623: 99% smaller. Registered plots are re-checked 44 times faster, and new boundaries about five times faster. A plain GeoTIFF of the same codes is already 98.6% smaller, so the win is the layer analysis.

What it finds · 10 sHansen alone flags 7 of the 17 plots for loss after 2020. Against JRC's 2020 forest map, only P-0001 shows mapped loss on mapped 2020 forest: 2 cells in 2022. The demo shows this plot.

Caveat"44× faster" is a hot, per-plot time for plots registered in advance, against the project's own first version. Registered plots and new boundaries are always stated separately, never as one blended multiple. The batch figure (about 16×) needs a batch in hand.
Go deeper: where each number comes from evidence

6.1 KB against 623 KB: 6,146 B against 623,237 B (docs/lab/decision-layer-report.md, "How much smaller"). 44× and 5.2× are the Phase 4 hot figures on the 17 supplied plots; 83× is the NumPy-free re-check; 410× is the answer table, a cache. The proof table is in the same report under "Same answers, proven for every cell". "Hansen alone flags 7 of 17" and P-0001's 2 cells are in docs/writeups/04-pitch.md and the report's "What the decision layer says about the 17 plots".
