2 · The company and the problem

Prove a farm plot was not cleared of forest. Again, and again.

Companies that sell rubber, palm oil, cocoa or coffee into the EU must show every supplying farm plot is deforestation-free. That is the EU Deforestation Regulation, EUDR. SystemEarth works with exactly this kind of plot-level evidence.

In practice you take a plot's boundary and ask satellite maps two things. Was the land forest on 31 December 2020, the rule's cut-off date? And has tree cover been lost since? The maps come from different sources at different sharpness, and they are updated every year. So the check is re-run for millions of plots, every time a layer updates, and the data piles up.

SystemEarth's question, and the title of their slide: "Keep the decision, not the data." Store the layers compactly, query them fast, and still reproduce exactly the same plot-level decisions as the original layers.

3 · The data

Seventeen plots, two public maps.

A raster is a map stored as a grid of squares, one number per square, like graph paper. Satellite forest maps are rasters. A farm plot is the opposite kind of thing: a vector, an outline traced with coordinates. The whole task is laying outlines over grids.

From SystemEarth

17 supplier plots

One shipment lot, one supplier. A CSV of 17 declarations (35 columns) and a GeoJSON of 17 matching outlines, in Chai Buri, Surat Thani, Thailand. Smallholdings of 0.53 to 20.07 ha. The two files join one to one.

Public · Hansen GFC 2025 v1.13

Tree-cover loss

Three bands at about 28 m: canopy in 2000, the year of loss (2001 to 2025), and a land/water mask. Our chip is 1,233 × 604 = 744,732 cells.

4 · What we built

Plotline: ask only what the rule asks.

Plotline starts by listing the three questions an EUDR-style screen actually asks of a plot, and what each one needs from the maps.

Q1

Was it forest on 31 Dec 2020?

Needs JRC's 2020 forest class, and land / water / unknown.

Q2

Has that forest been lost since?

Needs Q1 plus the Hansen loss year, 2021 to 2025.

Q3

What did the supplier's own check say?

The supplier named "Hansen Tree Cover Loss 2021-2023": any Hansen loss in 2021 to 2023, on any land.

Nothing here reads the canopy percentages, which are 72% of Hansen's bytes. Nothing reads loss years before 2021 either. So we dropped them. Each map cell is boiled down to one of 20 codes. Watch the original layers shrink.

The screening layer answers all three questions. The decision layer answers Q1 and Q2 only, so it also forgets the loss year on land that was not forest in 2020. That is loss on land the EU's 2020 forest map does not class as forest, which is what a Hansen-only check trips over. Here are the 20 codes.

3 forest states × 6 loss states + water + unknown = 20 codes. The decision layer merges every N·year into plain N. Orange outline = loss after the cut-off. Unknown is its own value: a missing observation never turns into a reassuring "no loss".

Go deeper: where the last bytes come from technical

A plain GeoTIFF of the decision codes is already 8,532 B, 98.6% smaller than the originals. So most of the win is knowing what to keep, not a clever codec. Our encoding (separate bit-planes for land, water and forest, plus a sparse list of the few loss events) adds the last 28%, down to 6,146 B. The lossless "v1" store stays as the audit tier if the rule ever changes.

A learned codec selector was tried on a sealed holdout region and lost to a fixed setting, so it was rejected.

6 · The proof

Same answer, checked for every single cell.

Dropping data is only acceptable if nothing the decision reads is lost. So an independent path rebuilds the code for every map cell straight from the original GeoTIFFs and compares it with the decision layer. Not a sample: all 744,732 cells.

Why that covers every plot, not just our 17: each answer depends only on the codes of the selected cells. If every cell's code matches, any boundary drawn in the region gets identical outputs under the same centre rule.

Read the small print

  • Hot is not end-to-end. Hot time is the query once data is loaded. A fresh Python process spends most of its time starting up and importing NumPy, so end-to-end barely moves. The one exception is the numpy-free re-check of registered plots: 50.0 ms down to 24.9 ms, because it never imports NumPy.
  • Registered and new are separate. The index and answer table only answer plots registered in advance; the 410× table is a stored answer keyed by plot ID, geometry hash and layer hash. New boundaries use the decision layer.
  • The batch needs a batch. One new plot gains nothing; on the 17-plot batch it is only 1.2× ahead of the one-at-a-time path.
  • Everything is relative to our own v1 path. Other EUDR tools publish no per-plot latency, so we make no "faster than" claim about them.
Go deeper: how it got from 24× to 44× history

The decision layers were built on the afternoon of 15 October and benchmarked once: registered plots 24×, new boundaries 2.2×. Four phases followed with no change to any output, store byte or file format. Phase 1 (15 Oct evening) sped up decoding, histogram reduction and polygon selection (31× and 3.0×). Phase 2 (night) added a scalar scan, a batch evaluator and the numpy-free re-check (33× and 5.0×). Phase 3 (late night) sped up the shared reducer (44×). Phase 4 (16 Oct, early morning) trimmed it again (numpy-free 65× to 83×; new boundaries read 5.2×) and ended with a research pass that found nothing that beats this approach for small exact plots on a CPU.

9 · What it found

Seven plots show loss. One shows loss on 2020 forest.

With Hansen alone, 7 of the 17 plots show tree-cover loss after 2020. But Hansen records any tree-cover loss on any land, and this is rubber and oil-palm country. Put the JRC 2020 forest map under it, and only P-0001 has mapped loss on mapped 2020 forest: 2 cells, in 2022, out of its 38 Hansen loss cells that year.

What the supplier declared

It does not line up

All 17 declare "Deforestation-free", checked against "Hansen Tree Cover Loss 2021-2023". Five (P-0003, P-0005, P-0008, P-0010, P-0013) record an "indication of deforestation", resolved by on-site verification. On that layer and our rule, none of those five shows loss in 2021 to 2023.

The other way round

Declared none, map shows some

Three plots declared no indication but show 2021 to 2023 loss on the layer they cite: P-0001 (38 cells in 2022), P-0016 (1 in 2023), P-0018 (1 in 2021). Neither the older Hansen release nor a different boundary rule explains the gap.

Say it this wayThese are questions for the reviewer, not accusations. The supplier's evidence files were not supplied, and they may have used manual review, another layer or a buffer. That is exactly why every answer needs a receipt.
CarefulDo not say "deforestation found". Say "mapped loss on mapped 2020 forest". This is mapped observation under a written rule, not a legal compliance verdict, and it excludes EUDR's 0.5 ha minimum area, the "able to reach 5 m" test and protected areas.

Say itPlotline keeps the decision, not the data: a 6 KB decision layer gives the same EUDR screening answers as 623 KB of original map layers, proven for every cell in the region.

The pitch is a short story: the rule gets re-run, so most of the data is not needed; here is what the decision reads; here is proof for every cell; here are the honest numbers; here is what it finds. The judges are engineers, so stick to the numbers and the wording on this page.

Three things to be careful about: keep registered plots and new boundaries separate (never one blended "N× faster"), say "hot" when you mean hot, and call supplier declarations declarations.
