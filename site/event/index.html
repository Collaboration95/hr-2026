The event and the brief.

Hack & Hire is a one-week build-and-pitch event run with real companies. Plotline is the project's entry for the SystemEarth challenge.

15 min readLevel: contextSources: docs/event_rules_and_open_questions.txt, docs/problem_statement_reference.txt, docs/transcript_rules_and_constraints.txt, docs/sustainability_kickoff_reference.txt, SystemEarth-track.md, README.md
In brief
  • Build period 10 to 17 October 2026; teams of one to three; finals on 17 October, with a pitch directly to the company team.
  • SystemEarth's brief asks for a layer analysis, an encoding strategy, a working prototype and a comparison with the original layers on storage, query speed, resource use and decision fidelity (the share of plots whose outputs all match).
  • No pitch length, submission place or deadline, scoring weights or passing thresholds have been given.
1 · The event

Hack & Hire Sustainability Edition 2026

The event is organised by Projet with NUS CAT. Each team picks one of six real company challenges, builds a demonstrable slice over the week, and pitches it directly to that company's team at the finals. The repository records the aim as a practical outcome with clear reasoning and evidence.

Teams have one to three people. Entry is through a Projet profile (name and email); no CV is required. Participants rank three preferred challenges on a form, and the organisers allocate tracks and teams afterwards.

3 · The six challenges

Six company challenges

Cards follow the booklet's order. Role fit and the one-line ask come from the printed booklet; targets and extra guidance come from the spoken briefing. Spoken targets apply to their own challenge only and are not universal judging criteria.

BlueNexus · operations

Water-plant efficiency

Role fit: data analytics, AI/ML, engineering. Use plant data (flow, pressure, energy, chemical dosing, equipment status, water quality) to spot abnormal or inefficient operation and give early insights that cut energy, chemical and water use, while maintaining treatment performance. A full industrial AI platform is not expected.

Open: whether teams get plant data at all, and which fields.

BlueNexus · ESG

Autonomous water-system impact

Role fit: ESG, data analytics. Measure the impact of BlueNexus systems and present it credibly in a format clients can use for ESG reporting. Exact impact figures are not essential if the method is explained and the format is usable. GRI was named as a possible reference.

Open: which metrics, which baseline (and how seasons adjust it), data access.

Bizsu · leads

Lead-generation and sales engine

Role fit: business development, AI engineering. Bizsu's ceramic net helps air conditioners cool faster on less electricity without modifying the unit. Build a scalable way to get qualified leads and sales. Spoken targets: email 100 relevant people, seek a 20% positive response.

Open: the audience. The booklet says homeowners; the speaker said Singapore SMEs.

Bizsu · content

Brand awareness through content

Role fit: marketing and comms. Explain the product, introduce the team and motivate people to act. The spoken brief says a strategy alone is not enough: produce actual content. Spoken target: 30,000 views.

Open: which formats count, and how and when views are measured.

beez-fm · building energy

Technology fit, visualised

Role fit: data visualisation. Show a building's energy profile, the matched efficiency technology, and why it fits, so owners and finance decision-makers trust the recommendation before agreeing to a zero-CAPEX pilot. The organiser stressed executing the plan, not only proposing one.

Open: where the building data is, its schema, permissions and timing.

SystemEarth · geospatial Selected track

Multi-layer, multi-year geospatial data

Role fit: geospatial, data engineering. Represent several map layers across time and space compactly, make them cheap to query, and still reproduce plot-level compliance decisions. The company supplied 17 supplier plots.

Open: pitch length, submission details, scoring weights, thresholds.

Reasons for choosing SystemEarth

The repository records that the track was selected on 10 October and that acceptance was reported on 11 October. It does not record the reason. The following is an interpretation:

  • It can be measured. Storage, query time and fidelity are all computed against an original, so the result is a proof, not an opinion.
  • It had real data. The company supplied a sample of 17 plots; other tracks list data access as an open question.
  • It allows existing methods. A new compression algorithm is explicitly not required, so effort can go to analysis and evidence.

5 · Sorting the asks

Required, suggested, bonus and unknown

The project rules keep these four categories apart, so that no threshold is assumed where none was set and no request is skipped.

Required

What the brief asks for

  • A specific use case.
  • Several layers across time and space, kept compact and cheap to query.
  • Enough information kept to reproduce plot-level compliance decisions.
  • Layer analysis: which attributes are essential, which can be simplified, summarised or dropped, grounded in regulatory definitions and cut-off dates.
  • An encoding strategy that combines layers and time steps, plus a working prototype that encodes a sample region and queries it (slides; spoken is softer).
  • A comparison with the original layers on storage, query speed and resource use, plus decision fidelity and, where relevant, pixel fidelity.
  • Spoken: explain pipeline choices, accuracy, information loss and any AI output.

Deck pp. 24 and 25, booklet p. 9, transcript.

Suggested

Allowed, not demanded

  • Free choice of dataset, region and use case.
  • Existing algorithms, models, formulas and encodings are allowed; a new compression algorithm is not required.
  • A smaller, complete slice is favoured over a sprawling one.
  • Project plan (not the company's): compare against a well-configured compressed original and count indexes in the compact size.

Transcript; SystemEarth-track.md for the project plan.

Bonus

Nice to have

  • Estimate infrastructure and processing costs to store, update and query.

The evaluation slide (p. 25) and the transcript both mark it a bonus. It was done at list-price assumptions (last step of the decoder).

Unknown

Not stated by anyone

  • Pitch length.
  • Submission format, place and deadline.
  • Scoring weights and the judging rubric.
  • Numeric passing thresholds. The slides give no numeric benchmark either.
  • Whether a working prototype is strictly required.
  • What 17 Oct 00:00 SGT closes.
  • Whether the supplied sample may appear in slides or a public demo.

docs/event_rules_and_open_questions.txt, SystemEarth-track.md. The aim that every output matches on every test plot is the project's engineering target, not a company threshold.

Decision fidelity is counted per plot

The slides define it as "% of plots where all compliance outputs match the original layers." The operative word is all: a plot with one wrong output out of several counts as failed. In the sandbox, each square toggles one output between matching and differing, and the plot-level score falls faster than the output-level one.

Source: definition from the kickoff deck, evaluation slide (p. 25). Plot names S1 to S17 and the outputs are invented for this sandbox; they are not the supplier plots. The measured result is 17 of 17 plots with every output identical, and 744,732 of 744,732 map cells identical, which implies the same for any plot boundary in the region (docs/lab/decision-layer-report.md).

Pixel fidelity and dropped attributes technical

Pixel fidelity asks how well pixel-level detail survives, "where relevant". It is measured as 100% on every attribute the decision reads. Attributes the decision does not read, such as exact canopy percentages in 2000 and loss years before 2021, are dropped on purpose and listed as dropped, never called lossless. The lossless version-1 store stays as an audit tier if a rule ever changes.

8 · Scope and open questions

Evaluation order, scope and open questions

  • Evaluation order: the slides' three questions are how much smaller, how much faster, same decisions.
  • Scope of the results: they are encoding and query evidence, not legal compliance findings. Supplier declarations are declarations, not proven violations.
  • Open questions: the logistics unknowns are collected in Submission readiness; the storyline is in the Presentation guide.
