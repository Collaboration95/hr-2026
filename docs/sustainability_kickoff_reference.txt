SystemEarth — multi-layer geospatial data (PDF pp. 21–25)
Geospatial / Data Engineering — Building Multi-Layer, Multi-Year Geospatial Data

Rule

- Present a specific use case.
- Represent multiple geospatial layers across time and space compactly.
- Support efficient queries.
- Preserve information needed to reproduce plot-level compliance decisions.

Decision context
For EUDR, assess forest presence at the 31 December 2020 cut-off.
For EUDR, assess deforestation since that cut-off.
Assess whether production on each plot is legal.
Relevant data include natural-forest baselines.
Relevant data include annual tree-cover loss.
Relevant data include near-real-time deforestation alerts.
Relevant data include protected or restricted-use areas.
Relevant data include land-legality layers.
Relevant decisions can include degradation, overlapping geometry, and ESG risks.
Plot polygons are checked against the layers.
The slides describe re-checking plot polygons as layers update.
The slides do not state re-checking as a required prototype feature.
The slides do not state this date as a general cut-off for all use cases.

Requested work (printed company guidance)
The company asks participants to:
Identify essential layers and attributes.
Identify layers and attributes that can be simplified, summarised, or dropped.
Ground layer choices in regulatory definitions for the use case.
Describe an encoding strategy that combines layers and time steps.
Encode and query a sample region in a working prototype.
Compare the approach with the original layers.

Evaluation (printed company guidance)
Compare storage needs with the original layers.
Measure query speed for representative plot-level queries.
Measure resource needs for representative plot-level queries.
Measure decision fidelity as the percentage of plots whose compliance outputs all match the original layers.
Assess pixel fidelity where relevant.
Estimating infrastructure and processing costs is a bonus.

Points to confirm

- The printed guidance requests a working prototype.
- Spoken guidance treats a prototype less firmly.

Open questions

- The slides do not mandate a programming language or dataset.
- The slides do not name a sample region.
- The slides do not provide regulatory definitions for a chosen use case.
