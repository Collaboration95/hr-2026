Context

SystemEarth and the EUDR: plot checks that are repeated.

SystemEarth provides EUDR and supply-chain compliance software. For each farm plot it overlays geospatial layers to answer EUDR questions, with 31 December 2020 as the cut-off date. The check is repeated whenever plots or layers change, which makes it a data problem.

In brief
  • The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) allows certain goods onto the EU market only if they come from plots not deforested after 31 December 2020. Plot boundaries must be supplied and checked.
  • Application dates, checked 16 October 2026: 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators, 30 June 2027 for micro and small ones.
  • The challenge brief asks for compact, query-efficient layers that preserve plot-level decisions. Plotline's results are screening evidence, not a legal finding.
1 · The company

SystemEarth overlays farm plots with map layers.

The challenge brief carries the footer © 2026 SystemEarth Pte Ltd. It summarises the company's work in one sentence: for every farm plot, it overlays several geospatial layers to answer EUDR questions.

The brief names these layers:

  • tree cover loss and deforestation alerts: annual loss, plus near-real-time alerts;
  • protected and restricted-use areas and land legality layers;

2 · The rule

What the EUDR requires

The EU Deforestation Regulation is Regulation (EU) 2023/1115. Companies that place certain goods on the EU market, or export them, must show that the goods did not come from land deforested after a fixed date and were legally produced.

What is covered

Seven commodities, plus products made from them: cattle, wood, cocoa, soy, palm oil, coffee and rubber (leather, chocolate, tyres, furniture and similar). The Commission lists them and notes a delegated act adding some palm oil derivatives, soluble coffee and frozen cattle tongues from 30 December 2027. Source: European Commission, environment.ec.europa.eu, checked 16 Oct 2026.

Geolocation of production plots

The operator must know the plot of land where each commodity was produced. For plots over 4 hectares (cattle excepted) that means a polygon outlining the boundary. For smaller plots a single latitude and longitude point can be enough. Coordinates need at least six decimal places. Source: Fairtrade guide on EUDR geolocation data, which states "Last update: 16 April 2026"; checked 16 Oct 2026. The 17 supplier plots in the sample range from 0.53 to 20.07 ha, and all are polygons.

"Deforestation-free" and the cut-off

"Deforestation-free" means the goods were not produced on land deforested after 31 December 2020; clearing before that date is outside the rule. Production must also be legal, which is why the brief lists land legality and protected areas.

What counts as forest

The forest definition used in this project comes from the JRC product metadata quoted in the layer notes: land over 0.5 ha with trees higher than 5 m and canopy cover over 10% (or trees able to reach those thresholds in place), excluding land predominantly in agricultural or urban use. Cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber and soya plantations are not forest, so a rubber or oil-palm block that is cut and replanted is not automatically "deforestation".

UnverifiedThe official EUR-Lex text returned no readable content on 16 Oct 2026. The forest wording above comes from the project's layer notes (docs/lab/decision-scale-notes.md), which call for a check against Article 2 before it is quoted.

Due diligence

Before goods are placed on the EU market, the importer (the operator) must collect information including plot geolocation, assess the risk of non-compliance, reduce any more-than-negligible risk, and file a due diligence statement in the EU information system. SystemEarth's site mentions fixing GeoJSON errors "before they cause issues on submission to the EU Information System", which fits that step. The plot check belongs to the risk assessment.

3 · The cut-off

Loss counts only on forest, and only after 2020

Two map layers do two jobs. One shows which land was forest at the end of 2020 (the EU Joint Research Centre's 2020 forest map). The other shows the year each map cell lost its tree cover (the Hansen Global Forest Change maps). Together they answer three questions.

Go deeper: two details of the cut-off technical

Year resolution. Hansen records the year of loss, so loss in 2020 cannot be told apart from loss on 31 December 2020. The project treats 2021 as the first year after the cut-off.

Loss before 2021 on forest. If the 2020 forest map says a cell is forest, an earlier Hansen loss in that cell does not override it (the forest may have regrown, or the two maps may differ). The 2020 forest map is the baseline.

4 · Why it is a data problem

Repeated checks across plots, layers and years

A single plot check takes the boundary, finds the map cells inside it, reads the forest and loss values, and answers. The workload comes from multiplication.

  • Many plots. Suppliers can register large numbers of boundaries.
  • Several layers. Forest baseline, annual loss, alerts, protected areas, legality.
  • Many years. A loss map gains a year with each release, and a release can also revise earlier years.
  • Re-checking. The brief says plot polygons are "re-checked as layers update year after year". A plot that passed last year is checked again.

Two workloads follow, and Plotline treats them separately. A new layer release means every registered plot is checked again against unchanged boundaries. A new plot means one new boundary and one check. The first can reuse the cells found for each plot last time; the second cannot.

5 · The user

Two intended readers: an analyst and a technical reviewer

Plotline's recommended direction (docs/planning/systemearth-product-direction.md; a recommendation, not a company requirement) names two readers.

Primary user

The analyst

A company analyst reconciling a supplier's due-diligence spreadsheet with farm boundaries and mapped observations. Question: which supplier claims need review? Needs each declaration beside what the maps show, plus a record of missing evidence.

Second reader

The technical reviewer

Someone at SystemEarth deciding whether a smaller representation can be trusted. Question: can the answer be produced from less data without changing the evidence? Needs the same answer from original and compact data, plus size and speed.

Not a risk scoreThe product keeps four things apart: input or join problems, supplier claim versus mapped observation, disagreement between source layers, and encoding mismatches. Unknown is never shown as "clear".

7 · Scope of the results

Screening evidence is not a legal determination.

Plotline reports mapped loss on mapped 2020 forest, under a written rule, on two public map products. This is evidence for a reviewer, not a finding that anyone broke the law.

What the results show

Preservation

The compact layer gives identical plot-query outputs under the documented rule, for every cell and so every possible plot boundary in the region. This measures preservation of the answer, not map accuracy.

A question for the reviewer

Declarations

Supplier statuses are declarations, not facts and not proven violations. Where they do not line up with the maps, that is a question for the evidence files. The terms "red flag" and "non-compliant" are not used.

What is not covered

Gaps in the rule

Not covered: the 0.5 ha minimum area, the "able to reach 5 m in place" test, degradation, protected areas (WDPA licence unclear) and land legality. Counts are cells, not hectares.

ScopeThese are mapped observations, not findings of deforestation, deforested hectares, accuracy or ground truth. The results do not establish supplier intent: the five "indication of deforestation" records cannot be reproduced from the layer they name, and the evidence files were not supplied. Terminology: docs/writeups/04-pitch.md.
UnknownWhether the sample may appear in a public pitch is unconfirmed. The plot boundaries and declaration rows are private, so this pack uses only synthetic shapes and quotes only aggregates.
8 · Open questions

Questions for SystemEarth

These come from docs/lab/company-next-conversation.md, an unsent discussion brief, not a statement from the company. It proposes one short review of one plot and one receipt as the smallest useful next step.

Question 1

What should the reference query reproduce?

Which exact historical layer release, spatial inclusion rule, forest definitions and missing-data rules produced the supplied results? For one plot, the project's rule finds no mapped loss in 2021–23 among 87 selected cells while its row declares an indication. This is a prompt for investigation, not an accusation.

Question 2

What evidence should the reviewer inspect next?

The sample gives opaque reference keys, not files. What do they point to, and what review step follows an unresolved one?

Question 3

Which workload should pick the representation?

One interactive plot, a batch of thousands, repeated fixed-cohort checks, or arbitrary regions after updates? A plain GeoTIFF of the decision codes is 8,532 B against the store's 6,146 B, so the best container depends on the operation.

Also open: allowed use of the sample in a public repo, demo and slides; the final commodity and region; submission and pitch logistics.
